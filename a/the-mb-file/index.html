Delete, Deny, Repeat: Abdul El-Sayed's Greatest Reverse Hits

Michigan picks a senator Tuesday, Nov. 3. The Democrat who said "we do need to defund the police" says he "never, never" did. He wanted super PACs out of primaries until one funded by his father-in-law spent millions on his. Obama's presidency was a "list of failures" until Obama endorsed him. He signed a 2012 letter urging Egypt's Muslim Brotherhood-backed president to "purge the media," and he won't explain it. Here is every reversal, every family tie the press has reported, and every date.

By FNN Desk · Nov. 2, 2026

Watch: The MB File (video, 9.7 MB)

Abdul El-Sayed is a physician, an epidemiologist, the former head of the Detroit Health Department, the author of a book called Medicare for All: A Citizen's Guide, and the Democratic nominee for the U.S. Senate seat Gary Peters is giving up. Above all, he is a man who deleted his entire X history before launching his campaign, signed a letter asking a Brotherhood-backed president to purge the press, and now calls every question about either one an attack. We now know why he hit delete.

Election Day is Tuesday, Nov. 3. Republican Mike Rogers is the opponent. A Mitchell Research and Communications poll, exclusive to CBS News Detroit and published Oct. 28, has El-Sayed ahead 51% to 44% among likely voters. That means Michigan could, on Tuesday, send to Washington a man whose positions come with an expiration date and whose past comes with a delete key.

So we checked the dates. Every quote below has a date. Every date has a link. Where a quote comes from a transcription, a translation or a single outlet, we say so.

1. Police: From "We Do Need to Defund" to "Never, Never"

THEN: June 23, 2020, WDET interview with Jake Neher (around 7:45) "I believe that we do need to defund the police in so far as defunding the police is disinvesting in the means of incarcerating someone or killing them on the streets…" "…And that means both investing more in these services, and it also means investing less in police." CNN KFile, July 7, 2026, from the WDET audio

The same month, on X (since deleted, as reported by CNN KFile, Nov. 10, 2025): "The police have become standing armies we deploy against our own people." And: "#GeorgeFloyd and hundreds of other Black folks are murdered because our police departments are OVERfunded…take from one and give to the other." CNN counted about a dozen defund posts. All of them disappeared when he deleted his entire account history before launching his Senate run in April 2025.

NOW: November 2025 and October 2026 To The Detroit News: "I want to be clear, I actually never, never called for defunding. My goal in that conversation was to help everybody to understand what we were talking about." Bridge Michigan, July 17, 2026 On X, Oct. 10, 2026: "I support investing in local law enforcement, prosecuting violent criminals, and I spent my career in public health helping law enforcement make communities safer." On why the tweets vanished, to CNN's Kasie Hunt: "I deleted all the tweets because I didn't want them to be taken out of context like this…" CNN KFile, July 7, 2026

The gap: He said the words "we do need to defund the police" into a radio microphone. He tweeted that police departments were "OVERfunded." Then he deleted the tweets and told The Detroit News he "never, never" called for it. Bridge Michigan went through the record and found "ample evidence of El-Sayed's past support for defunding the police." Now his campaign swears he believes "we absolutely need to fund law enforcement." The 2020 El-Sayed said it on the radio. The 2026 El-Sayed says he never said it. One of them is lying, and they share a Social Security number. He didn't delete those tweets to protect the "context." He deleted them to protect the candidate. Deleting the evidence doesn't put it in context. It puts it in the trash.

2. Money in Politics: Ban Super PACs, Except the One Spending Millions on Him

THEN: June 17, 2025, on X "MAGA billionaires shouldn't be allowed to buy democratic primary elections—or any elections for that matter. Thank you Senator @BernieSanders for leading the way and calling for an end to super PAC spending in Democratic primaries."

Aug. 3, 2026, on X: "No corporation or corporate PAC should be able to buy a seat in Congress." JTA, July 7, 2026, described "the candidate's pledge to refuse all super PAC funding."

NOW: the FEC record Fighting for Michigan PAC is registered with the FEC as an independent-expenditure-only committee: a super PAC. PolitiFact reports it was set up last September to support El-Sayed. FEC records show $100,000 from Tayeb Jukaku on each of Feb. 3, March 31 and June 22, 2026, for a total of $300,000. The Washington Free Beacon identifies Jukaku as El-Sayed's father-in-law and the PAC's largest individual donor (WLNS reported the same relationship). By FNN's count of FEC filings through Oct. 14, the PAC has reported about $6.2 million in spending supporting El-Sayed.

Asked about it at the final primary debate, July 27, 2026: "My father-in-law does not want to send your tax dollars to the Israeli military to do genocide, I'll tell you that much." CBS News, July 27, 2026

The gap: He thanked Bernie Sanders for trying to get super PAC money out of Democratic primaries. Then he won a Democratic primary with millions in super PAC spending behind him, with $300,000 of that PAC's money coming from his wife's father. Asked about it on a debate stage, he didn't explain a dime. He changed the subject to genocide. Haley Stevens put it plainly, per the Free Beacon: "Who is putting money in yours? What are you hiding?" He still hasn't said. "Money out of politics" is on his yard signs. His father-in-law's money is in his super PAC. That isn't a campaign-finance philosophy. It's a family business with a slogan.

3. Medicare for All: From "Replacing the System" to "Can Still Be There for You"

THEN: July 31, 2019, Democracy Now!, the night after a CNN debate where Jake Tapper said Medicare for All would take private insurance away from more than 150 million Americans: "This is about replacing the system that we have, that has been corrupted by a set of corporations…" "It's not about taking away; it is about replacing with better." "I ran on a single-payer plan for Michigan."

The Detroit News (July 25, 2026) reports he has said he would co-sponsor and vote for the Sanders Medicare for All Act, which "would bar private coverage for health services that 'duplicate' anything included in the universal Medicare plan."

NOW: 2026 Jan. 22, 2026, Brian Tyler Cohen interview, clip posted to his X account (0:05): "If you like your insurance from your employer or from your union, that can still be there for you." To Michigan Advance he said he "shifted positions" because some union members didn't want to lose their plans. Oct. 11, 2026, Meet the Press: "If people want redundant care, absolutely. I don't want to take anything away…" Asked whether people can keep their plan if they like it: "Yes."

The gap: In 2019 the plan was to replace the system. The bill he says he'll co-sponsor bans private coverage that duplicates Medicare. In 2026, your union plan "can still be there for you," redundant coverage is fine, and yes, you can keep your plan. His primary rival Mallory McMorrow called it "rewriting definitions to have it both ways." Health policy experts told Michigan Advance it is still a version of Medicare for All, which means it's a version that shuts down the duplicate coverage he says you can keep. He can't have it both ways, so he's selling both and hoping nobody reads the bill he promised to co-sponsor. And when Meet the Press reminded him that Obama regretted "if you like your plan, you can keep your plan," he answered "Yes" anyway. Bold choice for a man who says he's read everything Obama wrote twice. (See No. 4.)

4. Obama: From "List of His Failures" to "Read Everything He's Written Twice"

THEN: Nov. 7, 2012, his HuffPost op-ed (bylined as an assistant professor of epidemiology at Columbia) "Obama was not the right choice -- he just wasn't the wrong one." "A Romney presidency would have been an utter disaster for all of the reasons that made Obama's last four years a failure." On the Affordable Care Act: "…it's a watered-down reform that kowtows to the interests of healthcare lobbies…" "This leader has perpetuated war, violated our civil liberties, deported more people from our shores than any other president before him, and the list of his failures goes on."

NOW: October 2026, with Obama joining him on the trail Oct. 11, Meet the Press: "I can't say how much I appreciate President Obama. I've read everything he's written twice…" And: "I am so grateful to have been a young man in my 20s with Barack Hussein Obama as president." He also said that back then he "was younger, right. And I was angry…" Oct. 12, on X: "President @BarackObama showed me something in myself I might never have seen without him. I'm humbled to have his support in this fight. The Affordable Care Act will have laid the groundwork for guaranteed healthcare, and I'm honored to pick up his baton…"

The gap: In 2012 the ACA was "watered-down" and kowtowed to the lobbies. In 2026 it "laid the groundwork," and he wants to pick up the baton. In 2012 he listed Obama's "failures" on war, civil liberties and deportations. In 2026 the man is a literary hero worth reading twice. He blames it on being young and angry. He was a Columbia professor publishing under his own name. What changed between 2012 and 2026 wasn't Obama's record. It was who could endorse him. His principles didn't evolve. They got a better offer.

A man who told a room he "carr[ies]" a second citizenship, then says he never did, is asking voters to trust whichever version is on the ballot. Even his citizenship had a THEN and a NOW.
