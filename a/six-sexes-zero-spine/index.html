Six Sexes, Zero Spine: James Talarico's Greatest Reverse Hits

Texas picks a senator Tuesday, Nov. 3. The Democrat who wanted to "tear down" ICE says he "never" wanted to abolish it. There used to be six sexes. Now there are two. Oil was a cassette tape. Now he "owes a lot" to it. Here is every reversal, with the dates.

By FNN Desk · Nov. 2, 2026

James Talarico is a seminarian, a former sixth-grade teacher, a Texas state representative (House District 50) and the Democratic nominee for U.S. Senate in Texas. Above all, he is a man who can face a camera in October and contradict, completely and calmly, what he said into a different camera in January.

Election Day is Tuesday, Nov. 3. Early voting ended Friday, Oct. 30, per FOX 7 Austin. The race against Republican Ken Paxton is a coin flip: 270toWin's average of nine polls, as of Nov. 1, has Talarico at 49.6% and Paxton at 46.2%, and the Quantus Insights poll of Oct. 26 to 28, reported by FOX 7 Austin on Oct. 30, has Paxton up 48.7% to 47.6%. That means the next senator from Texas could be decided by voters who never found out which Talarico they were getting.

We found out for them. Every quote below has a date. Every date has a link. If a quote comes from a transcription rather than an official record, we say so.

1. ICE: From "Tear It Down" to "I Never Have"

THEN: Jan. 24, 2026, Democratic primary debate Asked whether he would abolish or defund ICE: "It's time to tear down this secret police force and replace it with an agency that actually is going to focus on public safety." CNN, Jan. 24, 2026

He liked it enough to post it the next night, Jan. 25, 2026 (11:12 PM ET), on X: "It's time to tear down this secret police force." The post drew 574,319 impressions.

NOW: Oct. 14, 2026, CNN/Jubilee town hall, Fort Worth Asked by Erin Burnett: "Do you support abolishing ICE or not?" "No, and I never have. We need Immigration and Customs Enforcement in this country, but that's not what we're seeing." Houston Public Media/TPR, Oct. 15, 2026

The gap: He was asked whether he'd abolish ICE, and his answer was "tear down" and "replace." Then he was asked whether he'd abolish ICE again, and his answer was "never." He did not misspeak once. He said it at a debate, posted it on X, and, according to Houston Public Media, said it again in February. "Tear down" and "replace" is still tearing it down.

2. Biology: Six Sexes, Then Two

THEN: Apr. 20, 2021, Texas House Public Education Committee, hearing on HB 4042 "Modern science obviously recognizes that there are many more than two biological sexes. In fact, there are six, which honestly, Representative Hefner, surprised me, too, because I am not well-versed in this issue area. I'm not a scientist; I'm a politician, a lot worse than a scientist…" Transcription, Daily Wire, Apr. 26, 2021; FNN checked it against the hearing video

NOW: Fox News interview, published Sept. 9, 2026 "I've missed the mark on some of my past statements. I will be the first to admit it. What I meant is that there are two sexes, and then there's a small percentage of people with genetic abnormalities." "Obviously didn't say it correctly. There are not six sexes. There are two." Fox News, Sept. 9, 2026

The gap: In 2021 it was "obviously" six, according to "modern science." In 2026 it is "obviously" two. Same man, same word, opposite number. The only constant is the word "obviously," and the fact that he admitted he wasn't well-versed while doing it. He said it before he said the number, then said the number anyway. Biology did not change between 2021 and 2026. The electorate did.

3. Oil and Gas: From Cassette Tape to "I Owe a Lot"

THEN: September 2020, Sierra Club Austin virtual candidate forum "We all know that Texas has been a leader in oil and gas, but in many ways that's like being the leader in cassette tapes in the 1980s. That industry is not gonna last." CNN KFile, Sept. 1, 2026, from the forum recording

NOW: Oct. 14, 2026, CNN/Jubilee town hall "So I owe a lot to that industry, as most Texans do. And so I'm campaigning on all of the above energy strategy that includes oil and gas. I want to protect the good-paying oil and gas jobs that support whole families across the state." "And so I will stand up to my own party if they mess with our oil and gas industry…" CNN rush transcript, Oct. 14, 2026

The gap: Six years ago he was writing the industry's obituary in a Sierra Club Zoom. Now he's ready to fight his own party on its behalf. CNN notes that Texas oil and gas production has risen since he called it a cassette tape. The cassette tape outlived the prediction. So did the campaign's need for oil-patch votes.

4. Capitalism: From "Oppressive System" to "Proud Capitalist"

THEN: September 2020, same Sierra Club forum "I am so thankful that you asked this question and also thankful that young activists have really drawn the link between climate change and White supremacy and capitalism in our country. And I think only ending the ways that all three of those oppressive systems work together are we gonna be able to begin the process of dismantling them." CNN KFile, Sept. 1, 2026

NOW: September and October 2026 His campaign, to CNN: "James is a proud capitalist…" Spokesperson JT Ennis, CNN KFile, Sept. 1, 2026 Talarico, asked about it at the Oct. 14 town hall: "the problem with the media is they take one sentence out of a long conversation… I was talking about the system we have now, which is actually not capitalism. It's crony capitalism." CNN rush transcript, Oct. 14, 2026

The gap: In 2020 he put capitalism in the same "oppressive systems" bucket as White supremacy and talked about "dismantling them." In 2026 he says he meant crony capitalism, and he's a proud capitalist. "Crony" does not appear in the 2020 quote CNN published. Apparently it was implied, along with the rest of his current platform.

5. Trans Athletes: From "Problem That Doesn't Exist" to "I Oppose"

THEN: 2021 July 19, 2021, FOX 7 Austin, on Gov. Abbott's special-session agenda: "far-right conspiracy theories about trans children causing problems on sports teams, which we know does not occur in the state of Texas." As quoted by LGBTQ Nation; FNN checked it against the FOX 7 video

Oct. 14, 2021, Texas House floor, against HB 25 (which passed; he voted no): "If one trans child dies to protect someone's damn trophy, this bill is grotesque." MSNBC transcript, Oct. 15, 2021 In the same speech he called the bill an effort "to solve a problem that doesn't exist." FNN transcription of the floor video

NOW: Fox News interview, published Sept. 9, 2026 "I oppose trans athletes playing in girls' sports if it endangers safety or fairness." "And so, I will oppose trans athletes playing in girls' sports if it risks safety or fairness." Fox News, Sept. 9, 2026

The gap: In 2021 the issue was a "far-right conspiracy theory" about something that "does not occur" in Texas, and anyone who cared about the trophy was "grotesque." In 2026, the safety and fairness of girls' sports is suddenly worth opposing something over. The conditional "if" is carrying a lot. For the record, he was already making a local-control, "fairness and safety" argument in a February 2026 New York Times interview (via LGBTQ Nation). So the pivot came the moment he started running statewide, not last month.

6. The Life of the Mother: He Co-Sponsored the Exception, Then Said It Doesn't Exist

THEN (the record): May 22, 2025, Texas House SB 31, the Life of the Mother Act, clarifies that Texas's abortion ban has an exception when "the pregnant female… has a life-threatening physical condition aggravated by, caused by, or arising from a pregnancy." Talarico is listed as a House cosponsor and voted Yea on Record 3194, which passed 134-4. The law took effect June 20, 2025. Texas Legislature Online; Texas House Journal, May 22, 2025 (This card is his vote, not his words.)

NOW: Oct. 14, 2026, CNN/Jubilee town hall "the current abortion ban in Texas, which bans abortion in all cases, including in cases of rape and incest, including when the life of the mother is in danger." CNN rush transcript, Oct. 14, 2026

The gap: He co-sponsored and voted for the law that spelled out the life-of-the-mother exception. Seventeen months later, on national TV, he said the ban covers cases "when the life of the mother is in danger." Either the statute he put his name on does nothing, and he should say so, or the line he used in Fort Worth isn't true. If his real argument is that doctors still find the exception too vague, he should make that argument. The one he made is contradicted by his own vote.

The Closing Roast

Talarico's pitch is that he's the honest one, the seminarian, the teacher, the guy who'll "stand up to my own party." Look at his own receipts. In each of the six matchups above, the 2026 Talarico stood up to the 2020 and 2021 Talarico, and won. He's for tearing down ICE until he "never" was. He was for six sexes until he was for two. Oil was a cassette tape until it became a family heirloom. Capitalism was oppressive until it turned out he meant the crony kind. Trans athletes in girls' sports were a "conspiracy theory" until they were a "safety or fairness" issue. And he co-sponsored the life-of-the-mother exception, then went on CNN and said it doesn't exist.

At the town hall he said, "I already have a religion, and I already have a sports team." He didn't mention having a position. When asked about the "God is non-binary" line, he said, "I was being provocative, and I want to admit that and come clean about that." That's great. He can come clean about the rest on Tuesday.

Texans aren't voting on whether James Talarico is likable. They're voting on which version of him shows up in Washington, and right now, not even he seems to know.
