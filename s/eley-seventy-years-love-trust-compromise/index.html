“You Show Love to One Another”: Seventy Years On, Robert and Ida Eley Credit Morning Prayer, Trust and Compromise

And if one prevail against him, two shall withstand him; and a threefold cord is not quickly broken. Ecclesiastes 4:12

By FNN Desk · November 21, 2026

Robert and Ida Eley met as teenagers in Mississippi, married in 1956, and later lived in two states, he a union bricklayer in Chicago and she a pastor in Macon, Georgia. Morning calls, prayer, and room for each other to follow a calling kept them together.

Robert and Ida Eley met as teenagers in Brookhaven, Mississippi. “First time I seen her was at my grandma’s house,” Robert told WGXA in Macon, Georgia. “When I seen her, I just knew that was somebody that I liked.” They walked to and from school together, and their first movie outing was chaperoned by Ida’s little sister.

Robert joined the Navy and was deployed for months at a time. When Ida’s family moved to Georgia, he went looking for her. Just three days before he was set to deploy again, he took a Greyhound bus from Mississippi to Macon to ask for her hand. Ida was sixteen and told him to ask her mother and father. Her parents’ answer was easy, she recalled: “If he done did all that and come that far, he must mean business.” They married in Jeffersonville on September 26, 1956.

The Eleys moved to Chicago in 1960, raised two girls and a son, and Robert worked as a journeyman bricklayer. Then, in 1989, Ida said her faith called her back to Macon to care for her father and start an in-home church at his request. It became Eley’s Healing Temple on Houston Avenue, where she is now pastor. “The Lord sent me here,” she said. Robert stayed in Chicago to keep working. “I never interfered with anything that she said she wanted to do … and she didn’t interfere with my life,” he said. “That’s what kept us together,” Ida added.

Daily morning calls and prayer, and frequent visits between Georgia and Illinois, did the rest. “The Lord said he was there with you to do those things if you really believe it and trusted him,” Ida said. By 2000, Robert had joined her in Macon and become a deacon at her church. In 2004, their son, Robert Eley Jr., a Macon police officer and executive director of Macon Regional Crime Stoppers, died of a heart attack. Faith, the couple said, was key. “It don’t change or take nothing away; it just adds more strength,” Ida said.

Robert, now 89, and Ida, 87, live in Macon’s Unionville community with, in the station’s words, over a dozen grandchildren, including great-great-grands. “If you want everything your way, it ain’t gon’ work,” Robert said. “You show love to one another and do whatever you can for each other,” Ida said.
