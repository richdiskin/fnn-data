“God Will Not Abandon Us”: Five Years Apart Under Persecution, a Laotian Couple Is Back Together and Worshipping With Their Children

Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good. Romans 12:21

By FNN Desk · November 21, 2026

Kanya’s faith cost her her home and, for five years, her marriage, but she kept praying for her husband Phonesay. Open Doors reports that he came to Christ, they remarried, and the family now worships together at a local church in greater safety.

In the forests of Laos stands a church you would not recognize as one: a tiny space in a small hut, a plastic tarp for a roof, no plumbing or electricity, on government-owned land and unregistered. Each week a small congregation gathers there. Among them are Kanya and Phonesay, whose names Open Doors changed for security reasons.

They once lived in a village deeply tied to an animist belief system. Then Kanya fell ill with severe dental issues and nothing helped. “If you believe in God, He can help you,” someone told her. “I put my faith in God, regained my strength, and was healed,” she said. Phonesay had promised to follow if she was healed. “I thought, ‘If there is no persecution, I will believe later,’” he said. But persecution came quickly. Phonesay’s family expelled Kanya, and she was forced to leave the village while pregnant with their third child.

For five years they were apart. Kanya prayed constantly for their reunion and shared the Gospel with Phonesay by phone. “Every time we’d have a call, she’d share the Gospel with me,” he remembers. Others told her, “You believed in God, but you gained nothing.” “I said nothing. I just smiled. I believed we would reconcile because God is great.” Phonesay felt empty. “I wanted a new life,” he said, and he gave his life to Christ. Five years after persecution drove them apart, Kanya and Phonesay remarried.

When villagers came with hammers to tear down their house, Kanya sat and watched, choosing to pray and sing. “The Lord commands us not to repay evil with evil, but to overcome evil with good,” she said. The couple was expelled from the village. Other expelled families joined them, eight in all, on a stretch of land where Open Doors partners helped with construction supplies, food and Bible training. “We worshipped Him every day,” Phonesay said.

Open Doors reported on October 20, 2026 that Phonesay has since found a steady job, the family has moved to southern Laos, and they now worship with their children at a local church in greater safety. They are the only Christian family in their community, but letters of encouragement from Open Doors supporters reached them. “You don’t know how much this means to us,” Kanya said. And of everything: “God will not abandon us.”
