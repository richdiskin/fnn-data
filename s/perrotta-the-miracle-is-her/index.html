“The Miracle Is Her”: Told Their Daughter Would Not Survive, a Chesapeake Family Chose Life, and Lily Rose Is Now Five

I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made: marvellous are thy works; and that my soul knoweth right well. Psalm 139:14

By FNN Desk · November 21, 2026

Doctors told Aleesha and Sammy Perrotta that their eighth child, Lily Rose, would not survive, and at first suggested ending the pregnancy. They chose life, and five years later their daughter can write her name, dance with her sisters, and say the Our Father with her family.

In the early months of 2021, Aleesha Perrotta was driving to an ultrasound appointment for her eighth child when a radio story moved her. It was about a boy named Noah, born with only a small percentage of his brain, and the miracle of his life. She told the ultrasound tech about it. Soon afterward, doctors told Aleesha and her husband Sammy that their baby had a very rare form of skeletal dysplasia, and after weeks of testing, that their daughter was not expected to survive.

Doctors initially suggested ending the pregnancy. The Perrottas, parishioners at St. Stephen, Martyr, in Chesapeake, Virginia, made their decision very clear: abortion was not an option. They would choose life for their baby. Second and third opinions brought the same answer: “I’m so sorry, but your baby will not survive.” Aleesha carried her daughter with hope, received the Eucharist before her appointments, and found strength in prayer. “Our faith didn’t make any of it easy, but it helped us see things differently,” she said. “We trusted that God was with us. He was writing this story.”

Every night, the family and close friends prayed for strength, trust, peace, and for little Lily’s lungs to grow. Lily Rose was born ten days after the beatification of Venerable Francis Mary of the Cross Jordan, whose intercession they had asked. Aleesha chose a C-section so her daughter could be baptized immediately. Father Jim Curran baptized Lily Rose in the operating room, and, Aleesha recalled, the room full of doctors and nurses fell into a hush, hands folded in prayer.

Lily had an APGAR score of 1 and went to the NICU. Eighty-three days later, she came home. For a year and a half she was in hospice care. Today she is in homeschool kindergarten. She writes her name, paints at a Lily-sized desk her father made, dances with her sisters, makes the Sign of the Cross, and says the Our Father with her family. “Her lungs are small,” Aleesha said, “but she was made to be small.”

“Dignity is not something a person earns,” Aleesha told The Catholic Virginian. “It is in them because they are created by God.” “Our daughter’s value doesn’t come from the miracle. The miracle is her. We don’t love her more because she survived. We love her because she’s Lily.”
