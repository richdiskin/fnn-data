“No One Is Left Behind”: A Single Mother of Two Said Yes to Six Cousins, and Five Years Later All Eight Children Are Thriving

God setteth the solitary in families. Psalm 68:6

By FNN Desk · November 21, 2026

When children’s services needed a home right away for six of her young cousins, Yolanda Goodwin, a single mother of two, said yes to a phone call. Five years later she has adopted all six, and, in her words, “no one is left behind.”

When Yolanda Goodwin’s phone rang five years ago, she was a single mother of two with a vacation planned for the next day. On the line was the mother of six of Goodwin’s young cousins. Children’s services was removing the kids from the home, and they needed somewhere to go right away. “I’m going on vacation tomorrow, but sure,” Goodwin remembers saying. Overnight, her household of three became a household of nine.

Saying yes came naturally. Goodwin grew up in a big, close-knit family. Her mother lived next door to her grandmother, and the house was always full of cousins. Her grandmother had adopted too. The six siblings were not strangers; she had watched them grow up. At first she saw herself as a temporary safe place. “I’ll be the help until you get yourself together,” she said. “But the together never came.” When the county said the placement needed to become permanent, she did not walk away. “We just gotta work together. We gotta pull through this together.” She adopted all six.

Today her household includes her daughter Dariyonna, 19, and her son Camari, 12, along with Lorenzo, 13, London, 11, Harlem, 10, Royal, 9, Messiah, 8 and Prince, 5. Her own children call her Mom. The six call her Auntie, a deliberate choice, because their parents are part of their lives again. “I never wanted to take the spotlight of mom and dad,” she said.

The change has been dramatic. When the children first arrived, she said, they were wary of new people, and report cards were not looking their best. Now, she said, she sees “A’s, B’s, and maybe a couple C’s, maybe one D.” “Everybody is happy. No one is left behind.” One of the children summed it up: “My auntie, she care for us. She care for us like we’re her child.”

“My purpose is to make sure that they have enough love to be able to spread the love,” Goodwin told WKYC. Stacy Gura of Cuyahoga County’s children’s services agency, who called Goodwin inspiring, said caseworkers look to relatives first, and that about 300 children in the county’s permanent custody still need a permanency plan. Goodwin’s advice to anyone on the fence: “A good heart will take you there.”
